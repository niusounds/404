# The Gallery That Was Full of Dust

**Date of visit:** May 23, 2019, approximately 4:30 PM  
**Visitor count at entry gate:** 7 (including myself)

I didn't come to the museum because I was particularly interested in art. I came because my friend K invited me, and she said "the exhibition is almost over" with what I now recognize as genuine fear in her voice. She never did tell me exactly why it was almost over.

The gallery had been open for three weeks under the name simply titled 展示室 (Exhibition Room). No curator listed on any materials received. No artist names were printed anywhere. Each room contained only one painting, hanging alone on a pale blue-grey wall that seemed to absorb light rather than reflect it.

---

**Room One: "The Window That Wasn't There"**

The first piece was exactly as its title would suggest if it had one—at least according to the visitor log I later found at the security desk in Room Seven. A painting showing a window, viewed from inside a room lit only by moonlight through bare curtains. The paint was remarkably fresh-looking despite being described in some earlier reviews as "aged beyond recognition."

I stood there for what the other six visitors confirmed we each spent exactly eleven minutes on this piece. Eleven minutes is a long time to look at a window and nothing else. But when I finally stepped back, my friend K whispered something that wasn't about the art:

"It's only getting started. Every room adds another layer."

I thought she meant metaphorically. Of course I did.

---

**Rooms Four through Nine: Cumulative Progression**

Starting here, things became genuinely strange, not merely surreal but systematically wrong.

*Room for:** Painting 2—A child's hand holding a thread connected to nothing visible anywhere in the frame.* **Duration spent:** ten minutes.

**Painting 3 (third room):** A staircase ascending from below floor level. Each step contained a single word written in different languages, but the words progressed backward: "Here" → "there" → "somewhere beyond us." Spent approximately nine minutes on this one.

**Painting 4:** Two figures facing away toward an open door that opened onto another painting entirely—one within another—until you realize what's inside is just your own face looking back through layers and layers of recursive self-portraiture, each smaller than the last. The visitor log showed every single one (seven total) spent more time on this piece: 17 minutes for me; eight minutes by friend K who later told me she thought it looked like someone else staring at them from behind glass.

Something about that moment still bothers me in ways I can't fully describe because we were together, yet neither of us said the same thing afterward.

**Painting Five:** A table set for dinner—three places empty despite seating eight chairs around an oval mahogany surface with three place settings already laid out. Three forks gleaming under overhead lighting that shouldn't have existed within a painting. The air smelled like something cooking from somewhere not inside the frame at all, yet undeniably present near us while we stood before it: garlic and onion caramelized in olive oil with what might have become burnt rosemary if I hadn't been too afraid to lean closer.

I said nothing about this to anyone until much later because my voice failed me entirely when trying to explain "the smell was outside of the frame." My voice never truly recovered, though I could speak afterward normally again—just slightly hoarser than before.

**Painting Six:** An empty hallway extending far beyond what should fit inside any given canvas's dimensions (this was confirmed by three separate visitors standing back from it; measurements taken with a laser rangefinder at roughly five meters between nearest edge of frame and apparent vanishing point, though I cannot confirm accuracy since measuring equipment was confiscated upon entering).

**Painting Seven (Room 8): A mirror positioned so that its reflection captured everything except you. The painted figure in the mirror's perspective seemed slightly taller than reality—no explanation offered anywhere.* **Time spent here?** Eleven minutes exactly again; no one moved at once until they all realized simultaneously what was missing from their reflections.

---

**The Eighth Room — Where I Was Asked Questions Instead of Being Allowed to Answer Them**

I had never encountered such an explicit invitation before: not "Please don't touch" or "No photography permitted," but something written directly on the wall adjacent to where we stood looking inward at each other through mirrored surfaces, asking visitors their thoughts about what they saw staring back.

The question mark itself was painted inside quotation marks in a typeface resembling handwriting that looked slightly familiar yet wrong—like reading someone else's notes pretending not to be yours.

Then came actual responses written below it from strangers who'd come before, some entries barely legible because the ink appeared fresh enough to still be wet despite being layered over decades-old layers of previous visitors' scribbles. These included:

- *I think it sees only those already seen*, scrawled hastily in blue ballpoint pen near top left corner

*Look harder—I mean really look,* written below that in pencil

"Whoever painted this understands something about looking without being looked at themselves," added someone else entirely separately, positioned lower down, separated from both previous entries by a horizontal gap large enaugh for several sentences but filled only with whitespace.

None of these three individuals identified themselves by name. I asked friend K if she remembered any details—her expression didn't quite match concern as much as relief at being elsewhere—and she said simply: "I don't remember those particular entries anymore, did you?" As though whatever they contained had been erased from her memory just now along with mine perhaps moments ago before I could fully register them.

---

**Final Room: Empty Wall With Single Mark**

We exited through another door leading directly out onto sidewalk without ever reaching a conclusion about which room was considered "the last exhibit." There were no more walls visible beyond exit thresholds after that one marked simply as 最終室 (Final Room) though its contents defied description entirely except to note absence rather than presence—a blank wall interrupted only by what looked like someone had pressed something soft against it repeatedly, leaving behind smudges resembling fingerprints arranged deliberately enough for consideration but not clearly identifiable either way, perhaps because fingerprints themselves were missing altogether upon close observation—too much or too little depending on perspective—or possibly just dried paint residue from previous gallery installations having migrated into current space over time.

---

My friend K hasn't replied to messages sent since visiting together three weeks past now—but maybe that's coincidence given her busy schedule anyway; still, I find myself unable stop thinking about whether her silence means anything more meaningful connected specifically toward experiences shared during that afternoon walk through museum halls closed off permanently following our departure.
