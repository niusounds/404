Woke up in the middle of the night. The mother checks by breathing first.

She crawls barefoot down the hallway to make sure her four-year-old son Eirik is sleeping. When she opens the bedroom door — cold flood fills her lungs. Not winter cold. Wet breath from deep within a forest reaches her instead.

Moonlight touches Eirik's profile on his bed. It is too dark to tell whether his eyes are open or closed. But a mother knows — that breathing rhythm has changed.

The grandmother once told tales. The fae steal human children and replace them with changelings crafted from mud and rotting leaves. Listen beside the sleeping child when the moon is directly overhead, she said — you will know.

Mother kneels beside Eirik's bed, leans close.

Her breathing sounds are there... but they are not a breathing at all. Something else grates against the wooden frame. Wet dead leaves scraping one another. And through that sound comes, faintly, a voice.

It will soon be replaced.

She turns back. Through the curtains someone stands impossibly thin and tall, emerging from shadows like ink dissolving into water.

**The Horror Paradox at the Core of Scandinavian Folklore:**

Changeling tales contain an interesting mechanism: "the fae copy the child's face perfectly" is part of the lore. The appearance is flawless, yet the creature does not kill — only replaces it entirely.

This is pure parental anxiety codified into myth. When a baby won't stop crying at night, parents wonder: _is this really our child in front of me?_ That thought has circulated across Scandinavia for over twelve hundred years now. The damp cold from deep forest and the groaning wood are direct attacks on physiological dread. "The month when the moon is perfectly overhead" also mirrors boundary-lore in fae cosmology — days and times within certain thresholds belong to the Other World. The terror of these tales lies not in the act itself but in the interval the mother must traverse before realizing what she held for years was never hers, indeed, after all it cannot really stop being one's child no matter the truth staring back.
